# QuietQuorum — three-minute demonstration

Audience: hackathon judges. Narration: calm, warm, clear English; stock ElevenLabs voice, no imitation. Target 165–178 seconds, with breathing room before the three-minute limit. All interviews are fictional. Show genuine application footage; label any accelerated model waiting and pre-existing generated findings.

## Narration

Imagine asking six people when a community workshop should happen. Four prefer Saturday. Two cannot attend. A short summary can make the majority sound like everyone—and leave those two people out.

QuietQuorum helps community organizers, volunteer researchers, and small nonprofits turn interview notes into findings they can check. It keeps original notes on the organizer's computer, and makes space for the voices that disagree.

Let's follow a fictional workshop study from both sides: the organizer who needs a useful decision, and the participant who wants to be understood.

First, the organizer records an interview. Private names and contact details have separate fields. A participant says, “I cannot attend on Saturdays because I work then.” That sentence should survive the summary. Their contact details should not travel with it.

Now we disconnect the app from the network. The organizer can still capture notes and create evidence cards. On a compatible computer, with the model already downloaded, Firebase AI Logic can suggest exact excerpts on the device. Original notes never automatically fall back to cloud processing. Missing passages remain visible for manual review.

Before anything is shared, the organizer reviews each excerpt, removes identifying details, and approves it. Editing an approved excerpt clears its approval. The next screen shows the exact outgoing question and evidence. Private identity fields and unapproved text are excluded.

Back online, cloud Gemini compares only those approved excerpts through Firebase AI Logic, protected by App Check. Here, the Saturday finding shows four supporting participants and two who cannot attend. One person repeated their preference, but the app counts that person once.

From the interviewee's perspective, this is the important moment: “My needs are still here, even when most people want something else.” Clicking a citation opens its original local note. Another participant only has a phone; their concern about a laptop requirement remains visible too.

The organizer also checks evidence left outside the findings. AI can misinterpret agreement or conflict, so every result is a draft requiring human review. The current prototype does not replace the researcher's judgment.

After review, the organizer can export a report containing approved evidence and pseudonyms. Changing the evidence blocks an outdated export. QuietQuorum helps people make better-informed decisions by keeping the source, the privacy boundary, and the exceptions in view.
