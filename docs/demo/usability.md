# Prospective-user study

Recruit two or three people who facilitate consultations, coordinate nonprofit programs or analyze interviews for a small community organization. Use synthetic interviews. Participation is voluntary. Ask permission before timing or recording anything. Record anonymous role descriptions and task observations only.

Give each person the fictional workshop and one follow-up interview from a returning participant. Ask them to find a viable time, identify someone it excludes, link the return interview, correct a questionable citation and prepare a shareable report. Do not explain the privacy model before asking them to predict what will leave the device. Offer help if requested and log each prompt.

| Outcome | Observation |
| --- | --- |
| Analysis time | Elapsed minutes from opening the study to a reviewed report. A time-saving claim requires a matched comparison task and counterbalanced order. |
| Source trace | Time and actions needed to locate the exact sentence supporting a finding. |
| Conflicting evidence | Whether the person notices a minority constraint without prompting and explains how it changes the decision. |
| Count confidence | Predicted distinct-person count before and after linking the follow-up. Record the explanation and confidence from 1–5. |
| Privacy understanding | Ask what stays local, what is sent on confirmation and what appears in the exported file. Compare predictions with previews. |
| Correction | Whether they can change a citation role and understand why review resets. Record errors and assistance. |

After the task ask: Which step felt least clear? Which evidence changed your conclusion? Would you be comfortable sharing this report, and what would you remove first? What would prevent use in your current research process?

Report observed counts with denominators, participant roles, task timing and assistance. Keep comments anonymous. No prospective-user sessions or adoption claims are recorded for this release.
